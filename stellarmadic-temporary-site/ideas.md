# StellarMadic Temporary Site — Design Exploration

| Theme Name | Very Brief Intro | Probability |

## Chosen approach: Modern Observatory

### Design Movement

**Neo-editorial astronomy**: contemporary editorial design informed by observatory instruments, archival star charts, and restrained luxury identity systems.

### Core Principles

1. Treat the temporary page as a confident introduction, never as an apology or an error screen.
2. Use asymmetry, generous negative space, and a single focal visual to create authority in a compact page.
3. Balance atmospheric depth with sharply legible information so the page serves an interview audience immediately.
4. Use precision marks, hairline rules, and small navigational labels to make the experience feel intentional.

### Color Philosophy

The page is built around an almost-black **astral blue** that signals depth and focus, with bone-white typography for high-contrast clarity. A restrained brass accent represents the brand's forward motion and gives calls-to-action a recognisable, ownable warmth.

### Layout Paradigm

A deliberately off-centre editorial spread: a narrow information rail anchors the left edge while the headline and celestial visual occupy an expansive, shifting field. The layout avoids a conventional stacked card grid.

### Signature Elements

1. Fine orbital arcs and star coordinates that travel behind the hero content.
2. A small four-point stellar mark used as the brand emblem and page favicon.
3. Brass rules and numbered micro-labels that organise the page like a research folio.

### Interaction Philosophy

Interactions remain quiet and deliberate. A subtle star-field parallax responds to pointer movement; buttons tighten and brighten on hover rather than bouncing or glowing excessively.

### Animation

The hero visual drifts only through slow opacity and transform transitions, while content enters in a brief, staggered upward reveal. All non-essential motion is disabled for users who request reduced motion. Animation stays under 300ms for direct interactions.

### Typography System

**Cormorant Garamond** provides the large, expressive editorial display voice; **DM Sans** provides precise, readable labels and body text. Headlines use tightly tracked semibold serif text; labels use spaced uppercase sans-serif at a small scale.

### Brand Essence

**StellarMadic is a distinctive new point of view, prepared for ambitious conversations and meaningful next steps.**

Personality: **considered, luminous, self-possessed**.

### Brand Voice

Headlines are concise and aspirational; calls-to-action are clear and personal. Avoid filler and generic launch language.

> “A new point of view is taking shape.”

> “Begin a conversation.”

### Wordmark & Logo

The wordmark pairs a high-contrast serif “Stellar” with a quieter sans-serif “Madic.” The visual mark is a four-point compass-star with an offset orbit, suggesting direction rather than decoration.

### Signature Brand Color

**Observatory Brass — #C79A55**

## Style Decisions

The portfolio expands the chosen **Modern Observatory** system into a proof-led personal site. The landing view must establish Christine Urban as a customer-facing technical operator, not a generic personal-brand personality. The hero uses a direct problem-to-outcome statement; the body moves through observable evidence, selected delivery stories, hands-on builder work, and a future-facing crypto and AI perspective. Project cards should read like compact field notes—role, context, action, and outcome—rather than glossy case studies with invented metrics. The technical-to-nontechnical translation skill is a structural theme: each section pairs precise evidence with plain-language consequences.

The hero will carry a visible instrument diagram—nested brass orbital rings, coordinate marks, and the StellarMadic star—so the first screen is unmistakably an observatory system. Pale sections retain the same system through faint chart lines, brass coordinates, micro-labels, and branded folio marks. Enterprise work will use an explicit **context → action → consequence** annotation hierarchy, making the portfolio’s evidence feel like a field log rather than a conventional case-study list.
